# Chapter 29. Dialect, register, social variation and linguistic equality

## Foundations and definitions

Languages vary across geography, age, occupation, interaction setting, community and speaker identity. Dialects are systematic linguistic varieties, not defective versions of a single ideal language. A standard orthography often reflects historical institutions and power relations rather than scientific superiority. Register differences may include lexical choice, pronunciation, grammatical construction and discourse expectations. Comparing varieties requires careful sampling, community context and awareness that one person can shift styles. Writing only the standardized version of a form can erase important history and culture from a future knowledge archive.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A study may represent features at the level of individual utterances nested within speakers, communities and time periods. Treating every utterance as independent while ignoring speaker or family clustering can inflate the effective sample size. A model trained heavily on one variety may be inaccurate or misleading on another. Evaluating fairness requires a declared task, a legitimate label, representative data and an independently reviewed social impact assessment. Language tags and dialect names are descriptive metadata, not verified demographic identities.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Construct a small toy sample where one speaker contributes many repeated utterances and another contributes one. Compare naïve token-weighted frequency with speaker-weighted frequency, explaining why each answers a different question. Keep variation as an observation rather than calling a variety “incorrect”.

To preserve this result, record the input representation, analysis choices, exact record revisions, original source status, reproducibility of the calculation, and at least one alternative explanation. Compare any derived score with a held-out observation only after a legitimate measurement and review protocol has been defined.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
