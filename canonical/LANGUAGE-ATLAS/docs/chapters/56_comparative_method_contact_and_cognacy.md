# 56. Comparative linguistics: cognacy, borrowing and repeated sound correspondence

The comparative method investigates historical relationships by looking for systematic correspondences across a sufficiently broad body of lexical and grammatical material. Similar-looking words are not automatically cognates. Resemblance may arise through chance, universal tendencies, expressive sound symbolism or borrowing. Language classification also depends on contact, areal convergence, scribal traditions and incomplete records. Counting similar strings as proofs of common origin is particularly dangerous in a knowledge vault that may be used without specialist teachers.

## Reconstruction

Begin with well-documented lexical items and meaning slots, including source, date, language variety and any known phonemic transcription. Assemble hypothesized cognate sets and record who proposed them, what alternative hypotheses exist and why. Align corresponding segments *within a candidate cognate set*, not arbitrary words. Search for repeated correspondences across independent lexical items. Establish chronological and geographical plausibility; check known borrowings and irregular changes; do not rely on a single high-scoring pair.

The `weighted_segment_alignment` example can attach an explicitly chosen low substitution cost to one proposed segment correspondence. A low edit score says only that one computational cost function prefers that mapping. It does not determine etymology, ancestry, or language relatedness. The staged sound-change tool can formalize proposed changes and generate predicted forms, but its predictive failures must be retained rather than edited away.

## Counterexample and uncertainty

Words with common short sound patterns can appear similar across unrelated languages; many candidate comparisons generate false positives. A family tree is a scientific hypothesis that can be contradicted by new data or contact history. WALS/CLDF-derived typological associations may be strongly genealogically or areally correlated; treating every language as an independent statistical trial inflates certainty. Preserve family, area and source distinctions, and do not equate a typological cluster with descent.
