# Chapter 27. Discourse coherence, information structure and repair

**Area:** Pragmatics

## Foundations and definitions

Discourse is more than a sequence of independently decoded sentences. Participants track topics, presuppositions, given and new information, discourse relations, and unresolved references. Coherence can involve explanation, contrast, cause, elaboration or temporal sequence, but surface markers are neither necessary nor sufficient for these relations. In conversation, repair occurs when participants detect or anticipate communication trouble and renegotiate wording or reference. Repair is a normal feature of interaction, not always evidence of cognitive failure.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A discourse annotation should distinguish the observable turns from analyst-proposed links and keep alternative coherence relations when evidence is insufficient. A relation like “because” in language does not by itself establish a causal relation in the world; causal identification requires separate assumptions and evidence. Text summaries can omit a necessary qualifier or contradiction, so a research library should retain a trace to source turns and indicate whether the summary was checked by a participant or independent reviewer.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Create a short constructed three-sentence explanation containing “because” and “however.” Identify candidate discourse links, then change the order to show how coherence or reference may shift. Label the causal relation in the text separately from real-world causal proof.

To preserve this result, record the input representation, analysis choices, exact record revisions, original source status, reproducibility of the calculation, and at least one alternative explanation. Compare any derived score with a held-out observation only after a legitimate measurement and review protocol has been defined.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

This edition offers no full discourse parser or validated repair model. The connection to Causal Atlas is conceptual; language-level causal words do not establish intervention effects.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
