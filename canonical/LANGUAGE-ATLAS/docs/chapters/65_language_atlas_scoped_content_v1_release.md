# 65. Language Atlas v1: content sufficiency and the unfinished scientific gates

A scoped Language Atlas content milestone means that future readers have a coherent, navigable body of original explanation, mathematics, reconstruction procedures, failure cases, sources and evidence limitations. It does **not** mean every human language has been documented, that the Atlas can speak or understand them all, or that every linguistic theory has been independently confirmed. Natural languages and their communities are diverse and constantly changing; this archive is a substantial reference program rather than a completed census of human communication.

## What this edition teaches

The reference moves from phonetics/phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and pragmatics through signed and spoken multimodal communication, writing systems, typology, language acquisition, historical reconstruction and community documentation. Its new executable methods teach finite-state acceptance, explicit scope ambiguity under three-valued semantics, weighted string/segment alignment, stage-ordered changes, reproducible character n-gram scoring and rights-aware document accession. All these are bounded models with constructed examples and negative controls, not actual-language benchmarks.

## Graduation and accountability

Before declaring content scope v1, require complete numbered chapters, exact-record source closure, tested reference code, independent numerical comparisons where practical, data-rights classification and a working offline restore path. Run all nine legacy and new node suites without losing historical revisions. Mark `content_scope_ready` separately from `independent_linguistic_science_accepted` and `authentic_community_reviewed`.

Outstanding gaps include Unicode 18 full conformance tests and native property tables, original rights-cleared source bytes, authenticated field corpus and multimodal media, genuine speaker and community reviews, controlled translation evaluations, and multilingual model training or benchmarking. These cannot be inferred from unit tests, source links or an authored manual.

## The program-level order

This domain can eventually graduate its declared reference content and hand off to Energy Atlas, the next queued domain, while preserving the open scientific gates for future review. **All 30 domain libraries must be completed before Bitwork routing is developed or evaluated.** Integration tests and desktop transfer come afterward. A good knowledge archive must preserve unresolved questions and permission restrictions as carefully as its successful proofs.

**Status note:** this chapter is a prospective acceptance standard; independent review, real corpus ingestion and Unicode normative conformance are not claimed.
