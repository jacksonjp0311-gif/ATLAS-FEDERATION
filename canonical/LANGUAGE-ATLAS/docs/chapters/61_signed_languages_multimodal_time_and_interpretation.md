# 61. Signed languages, co-occurring articulators, and time-aligned evidence

Natural signed languages have their own lexicons, grammars and discourse conventions. They are not merely manual encodings of spoken languages. Hands, facial expressions, head movement, gaze, body posture and spatial reference can contribute simultaneously to meaning, so a flat sequence of written glosses may discard linguistically important structure. Annotation and comparison require time-aligned multimodal streams, not a single token list.

## Reconstruction

Represent a recorded event with source recording identity, language/variety, participant consent and access rights, annotation tier, start/end time with clock basis, annotator identity, gloss or description, and revision. Multiple tiers may overlap legitimately: a facial grammatical marker can span several manual signs. A global assumption that events never overlap would corrupt the evidence. Timestamps and frame indices must be traceable to the original media and its sampling/frame-rate metadata, and annotation revisions should be preserved rather than silently replacing historical readings.

The existing Language Atlas tier-audit method checks bounded time intervals and overlap patterns on authored metadata. It has not acquired authentic signed-language video or validated interpretations with fluent users. A correct time interval is not proof of semantic annotation accuracy. If access restrictions apply, a scientific preservation vault should keep metadata and a controlled pointer while refusing public distribution of protected media.

## Community and linguistic boundaries

Signed language and speech-language resources cannot be assumed mutually convertible by one universal tokenizer. Research needs linguistic specialists and communities, cultural/ethical review, and distinct evaluation procedures for comprehension, production and translation. The Atlas preserves these requirements as primary evidence obligations, rather than treating accessible captions or an avatar as a substitute for a full natural signed language.
