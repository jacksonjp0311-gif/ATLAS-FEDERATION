# 63. Prosody, information structure, and interlinear annotation

Prosody includes timing, intonation, rhythm, prominence and other suprasegmental organization in spoken communication; signed languages also use temporal and nonmanual resources. Prosodic cues can help disambiguate focus, question structure, phrasing and discourse status. An orthographic transcript often loses these cues. Information structure distinguishes, among other things, what is treated as given, contrasted or focused, but these analyses depend on language-specific evidence and discourse.

## Reconstruction

In a field record preserve original recording metadata, audio/video clock, orthographic transcription, phonetic transcription where available, lexical segmentation, interlinear morphological gloss, free translation, phrase boundaries, prosodic cues, relevant gesture/sign tiers, annotator and confidence/status. Interlinear Glossed Text should distinguish observed forms from grammatical hypotheses. Leipzig-style category labels have conventions, yet the existence of a gloss is not proof that the analysis is correct. Multiple annotators may disagree about stress, boundaries or morphology; keep their revisions and an adjudication trail.

A useful controlled example gives the same word sequence with distinct prosodic boundaries or focal stress and asks how a native speaker interprets it. The difference cannot be established by a written toy sentence alone. The v1 methods therefore cover metadata-level structure and explicit ambiguity, not acoustic pitch extraction or full multilingual prosodic parsing.

## Failure boundary

Automatic forced alignment can assign plausible timestamps to an inaccurate transcript. A waveform checksum only establishes equality to a stored blob, not that the annotation is linguistically true. Absent consent or unclear recording rights, do not publish raw media. Recording standards, clocks, codec versions, microphone conditions and dialect information should be preserved where available.

**Sources:** Leipzig Glossing Rules, IPA and Language Atlas multimodal annotation chapters; no real audio or speaker judgments were acquired.
