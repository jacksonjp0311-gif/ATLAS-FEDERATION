## Foundations and definitions

Words such as “I”, “here”, “now”, “that” and third-person pronouns depend on the circumstances of use or earlier discourse. The same sentence can denote different people and locations when uttered by different speakers. Anaphora connects an expression to another discourse representation, but the apparent antecedent may be ambiguous. Preserving language as knowledge therefore requires time, speaker roles, location conventions, conversation order, and enough context to interpret references. A detached sentence dataset can contain valid forms while losing the intended referents.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A context model might map indexicals to participant, place and time values and connect discourse variables through explicit assignments. Such mappings are hypotheses based on evidence, not universal rules inferred from grammatical gender or entity proximity. Coreference labels have their own uncertainty and bias, and pronouns are used differently across languages and social situations. If the relevant participant is unknown, an archive must preserve an unresolved reference rather than invent a person. This is an epistemic constraint as well as a grammatical one.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Compare the toy sentence “I am here now” with two different context records, changing speaker and timestamp. Then examine “They left after Alex called” without further context and list plausible antecedents instead of guessing one.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

No personal identity or location inference is performed here. The archive does not collect user-specific conversation history for these examples, and cannot resolve natural-language references without evidence.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
