# Chapter 02. Speech articulation and the production of linguistic sound

## Foundations and definitions

Speech uses airflow, voicing, and articulatory gestures by the respiratory system, larynx, tongue, jaw, palate and lips. Consonants can be classified by place and manner of articulation and voicing; vowels are described by tongue position, openness, lip rounding and other features. These labels are abstractions over continuous coarticulation, not fixed physical coordinates. Speech sound production also depends on individual anatomy, dialect, phonological context, health, and speaking style. Written spellings are not reliable phonetic recordings: a letter may stand for different phones or none, while one phone can have multiple orthographic representations.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A useful abstract phonetic token can be represented as a vector of independently declared features such as [voice], place and manner. A Hamming distance between two such vectors counts stipulated categorical differences, not physical acoustic dissimilarity or perceptual confusion. Some phonetic differences are continuous (formant trajectories, duration, fundamental frequency), and others involve timing and overlapping gestures. For a real measurement, record the microphone, sampling rate, environment and annotator; an IPA symbol is a transcription decision, not an instrument reading.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. ## Counterexample and failure boundary

IPA charts are an organized notation, not a claim that all languages use each symbol. No physiological or acoustic recordings were collected. Physical measurements require separate rights, instrument calibration and validation.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
