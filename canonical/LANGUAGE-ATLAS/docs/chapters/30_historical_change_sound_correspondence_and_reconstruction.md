## Foundations and definitions

Languages change through ordinary use, contact, demographic history, grammatical innovation and shifts in social convention. Historical linguistics compares related forms, regular sound correspondences, morphological structures and innovations to reconstruct plausible earlier stages. A coincidental look-alike word is weak evidence; a structured pattern across a sufficiently broad lexical and phonological sample is stronger. Language families are scientific hypotheses about genealogical relationships, distinct from people's ancestry or cultural value. Loanwords and contact can obscure simple family-tree models.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A comparative dataset must preserve source-language form, phonetic transcription, gloss/sense, date or variety, sound position, correspondence class and evidence status. A proposed proto-form is an inference whose uncertainty should remain explicit. A Bayesian phylogenetic model or distance clustering procedure can be useful under a declared stochastic model, but an unverified tree is not a historical record. A sound change may be context-conditioned, merge distinctions or interact with morphology. Source-based correspondences require independent documentation rather than generated transliteration guesses.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty.
