# Chapter 31. Language documentation, endangerment and community control

**Area:** Documentation  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

A language archive preserves much more than a wordlist. It may need consented audio or video, phonetic and orthographic transcription, translations, narratives, place-name knowledge, grammatical paradigms, interactional conventions and the circumstances in which materials were used. For endangered or underdocumented languages, the people whose knowledge is recorded should have meaningful authority over access, correction, cultural sensitivities and reuse. Some knowledge should not be public even if it is scientifically valuable. Preservation does not mean extracting every cultural practice without permission.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A provenance packet needs language and community identifiers with their self-designations, collaborator roles, recording date, medium, region where consent permits, equipment, annotation scheme, translation status, license or customary access restrictions, redaction and withdrawal procedures. Tiered access can protect restricted recordings while enabling public explanatory references. A machine-readable metadata entry should never silently upgrade “described on a website” to “original audio securely archived.” A rights-cleared, version-pinned source copy with verified SHA is different from a live URL or an AI-generated paraphrase.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Design a preservation accession form with fields for community review, contributor permissions, access level, raw media digest and annotation revision. Construct a toy record with absent consent and demonstrate that public release must remain blocked regardless of research interest.

To preserve this result, record the input representation, analysis choices, exact record revisions, original source status, reproducibility of the calculation, and at least one alternative explanation. Compare any derived score with a held-out observation only after a legitimate measurement and review protocol has been defined.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

The Atlas does not currently hold endangered-language recordings, community permissions or a curated primary corpus. Rights status must be checked before new archival ingestion.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
