# Chapter 35. Translation, equivalence and culturally grounded evaluation

**Area:** Translation  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Translation connects expressions across languages while preserving contextually relevant meaning and communicative function. There is seldom exactly one correct target sentence: tense, gender marking, evidentiality, formality, metaphor, politeness and information structure can require choices that the source did not explicitly encode. A bilingual word substitution table is not a translation system. Evaluations using a single reference string may reject legitimate alternatives; assessments should consider accuracy, completeness, register, context and the downstream consequences of errors. Community expertise matters especially for cultural knowledge and varieties with limited machine-learning data.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A translation record should include source text, language and variety IDs, source script and encoding, situational context, intended audience, translator/editor, target text, alternative hypotheses, back-translation where useful, and provenance of acceptance. A scoring procedure that measures token overlap cannot establish whether pragmatic force or factual content was preserved. Named entities, temporal reference, grammatical negation and quantities deserve explicit checking. A source without context may have multiple valid translations, and a responsible system should mark uncertainty instead of inventing the “correct” one.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Construct an ambiguous toy English request and list two plausible target-language interpretations as English descriptions rather than fabricated translations in an unfamiliar language. Explain what contextual or expert evidence would determine whether the utterance is a request, statement or irony.

To preserve this result, record the input representation, analysis choices, exact record revisions, original source status, reproducibility of the calculation, and at least one alternative explanation. Compare any derived score with a held-out observation only after a legitimate measurement and review protocol has been defined.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

No human translation study or real bilingual dataset was evaluated. The Atlas cannot certify translations into languages absent from its evidence; high fluency does not prove factual or cultural fidelity.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
