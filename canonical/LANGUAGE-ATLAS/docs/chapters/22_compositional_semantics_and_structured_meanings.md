## Foundations and definitions

Compositional semantics asks how the meanings of complex expressions depend on their constituents and structure. The principle is influential in formal semantics, but its exact interpretation is debated: idioms, context effects, lexical underspecification and pragmatic enrichment complicate simple combinations. A noun phrase is not merely a word bag; quantifier scope and syntactic attachment can change truth conditions. Distinguish lexical denotation, a compositional semantic object, the discourse referent and the speaker's communicative intention. A compositional semantic grammar is a mapping between a formal syntax and an interpretation, not evidence that the abstract predicates describe the world correctly.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A small logical semantics may assign predicates to sets or relations in a finite domain, then interpret conjunction, negation and quantifiers recursively. The assigned domain and facts are hypothetical inputs. Under open-world uncertainty, unrecorded tuples need not be false, so three-valued logic can preserve the difference. A semantic derivation requires variable binding and scope; attaching a quantifier in the wrong place can change the proposition even when the surface words remain identical.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. ## Reconstructive experiment

Give an invented domain of two people and a relation “reads.” Evaluate “Every scientist reads Ada” under explicit positive, negative and unknown tuples. Compare with “Some scientist reads Ada” and record which interpretations are not determined by the supplied facts.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

No machine-translated utterance receives trustworthy meaning merely because a formula was evaluated. The formal model must be justified by linguistic and world evidence separately.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
