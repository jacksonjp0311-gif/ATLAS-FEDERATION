# 57. Historical reconstruction, time depth, and unresolved alternatives

A reconstruction of an unattested language stage is an inference from surviving evidence, not a recovered audio recording. Linguists compare related varieties, texts, inscriptions, morphological patterns and regular sound correspondences, then propose ancestral forms that could explain observed descendants. The evidence varies by family, writing tradition and preservation conditions. The further back in time a hypothesis reaches, the more missing sources, language contact, changes in meaning and undetected variation can matter.

## Reconstruction protocol

Preserve every observed token with language variety, date/period, semantic context, transcription conventions, source and editorial normalization. Separate observations from reconstructed forms, give each reconstruction a stable identifier and list alternative explanations. Derive predicted daughter-language forms using explicit, ordered change rules and compare to withheld forms rather than selecting transformations after seeing all outcomes. Mark loans and irregular forms as competing hypotheses. A tree model alone may miss contact networks; source genealogies are not automatically independent.

The v1 code can replay an ordered sequence of toy changes and enumerate changes stepwise. It does not search for optimal historical hypotheses or infer the most plausible ancestral stage. Neither a weighted alignment score nor a guessed sound law has calibrated probability without an empirical model. Keep “unresolved” whenever multiple forms are consistent with surviving evidence.

## Survival implications

If a manuscript is only cited by URL and the source disappears, readers can still study the *method* from the original Atlas explanation but not independently recheck that particular manuscript's reading. Rights-cleared high-value original scans, critical editions and transcription notes should be acquired with precise provenance when possible; scholarly uncertainty must remain intact. A future linguistic researcher must be able to distinguish a true quotation from an editor's normalization or reconstruction.
