## Foundations and definitions

A phone is an analytically recognized speech sound; a phoneme is a unit of contrast within a particular language or variety. A minimal pair can support phonemic status when two forms differ in a targeted sound and differ in meaning, but uncontrolled spelling, tone, stress or morphological distinctions can confound the inference. Allophones are contextually distributed realizations of a phonemic category under a particular analysis. Contrastive and complementary distributions are useful evidence, not infallible algorithms that decide linguistic structure from a small word list.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A feature matrix abstracts possible oppositions. In a constructed system, /p/ and /b/ may differ by one voicing dimension, while [n] and [ŋ] may be linked by an assimilation rule before a velar consonant. Both analyses need corpus support and speaker judgment before being claimed for a real language. A phonological rule is a mapping conditioned by environment and grammar; a phonetic articulatory tendency can motivate it without making the rule universal. Rule ordering and representation choices can yield different apparent analyses.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. ## Reconstructive experiment

Use the explicit feature-vector tool to compare two constructed phonemes. Create three invented lexical forms, change only one segment, and say which additional semantic and speaker evidence would make them a genuine minimal pair. Then construct a rule that changes a nasal place label before a velar stop, and identify its assumed domain.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

Counting features is not a validated auditory distance or phoneme inventory discovery. Published PHOIBLE inventories use different analytical decisions; source dataset/version must travel with any comparisons.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
