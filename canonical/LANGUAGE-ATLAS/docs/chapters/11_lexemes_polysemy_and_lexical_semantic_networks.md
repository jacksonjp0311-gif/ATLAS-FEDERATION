# Chapter 11. Lexemes, polysemy and lexical semantic networks

## Foundations and definitions

A lexeme may have several related senses, and a single written form can represent unrelated lexical entries. Synonymy is generally context-sensitive, while antonymy, hyponymy, meronymy and other relations require explicit criteria. A dictionary entry is an editorial representation of usage, not a complete enumeration of everything speakers can mean. Word sense disambiguation depends on syntactic environment, discourse, shared knowledge and world context. Frequencies and embeddings can suggest associations but do not independently prove precise meaning or correct translation.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

Represent a semantic lexicon as typed relations between versioned sense IDs, not as an undifferentiated web of word strings. `bank` in a financial context and in a river context illustrates why word identity is not sense identity. A model should preserve unknown senses and evidence rather than treating a guessed gloss as certain. Even a correct sense relation does not prove that using it in a particular utterance matches speaker intent. Language change and social registers shift the applicability of entries.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Create a tiny fictional lexicon with two senses for one orthographic form. Add source contexts that favor each sense. Attempt to remove the contexts and observe that the word form alone cannot determine the sense. Represent one source's disagreement as a qualified relation, not a deletion.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

Lexical databases have their own rights, coverage and editorial assumptions. This edition does not reproduce copyrighted dictionaries or claim a comprehensive lexicon of any language.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
