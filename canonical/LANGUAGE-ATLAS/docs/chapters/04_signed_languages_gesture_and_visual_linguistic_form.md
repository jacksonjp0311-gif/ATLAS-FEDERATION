## Foundations and definitions

Signed languages are naturally occurring languages expressed through manual and nonmanual articulators. Handshape, location, movement, orientation, facial expression and bodily timing can contribute to linguistic contrasts and grammatical structure. Signed languages are not merely word-by-word renderings of the surrounding spoken language. Their grammars, discourse and sociolinguistic histories deserve primary attention, including Deaf communities' expertise and leadership. Gesture and conventional sign are not identical categories, and a mixed multimodal interaction may include both. A linguistic archive that collects only orthographic text has failed to preserve much of the communicative system.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A visual utterance can be represented as multiple time-aligned tracks. A symbolic gloss may help analysts communicate, but it loses kinematic and interactional detail. A video annotation requires time intervals, informed permission and a separation between observable gestures, analyst-inferred linguistic features and community translations. Nonmanual signals can overlap manual signs, so a forced one-token-at-a-time linear text model is not a complete representation. Meaning is constructed through syntax, discourse, context and agreed conventions, not a universal hand-gesture dictionary.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. ## Counterexample and failure boundary

This chapter contains no licensed sign-video corpus and claims no sign-language translation system. Consent, community governance and cultural context are central evidence requirements, not optional metadata.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
