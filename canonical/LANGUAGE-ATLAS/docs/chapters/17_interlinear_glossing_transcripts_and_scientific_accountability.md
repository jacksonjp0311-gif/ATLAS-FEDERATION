# Chapter 17. Interlinear glossing, transcripts and scientific accountability

**Area:** Documentation  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

An interlinear glossed text commonly aligns a source-language line with a morpheme-level analysis, a line of grammatical labels and a translation. The Leipzig Glossing Rules provide conventions for describing morpheme boundaries, clitics, grammatical categories and other annotation choices. Glossing is an *analysis*, not the primary sound/video observation; a different linguist may parse a form differently, and community members may reject an externally imposed category. An archive should retain source form, annotation tiers, analytic status, original orthography, recording metadata where relevant and the identity and consent status of contributors.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

Alignment is a relation among tiers, not necessarily a one-to-one mapping among code points. One morph may have several grammatical functions and several forms may realize one category. Hyphens, equals signs, periods, and underscores can have stipulated glossing roles, so a converter must not strip or normalize them without declaring the transformation. If a gloss line contains more segments than the source analysis, record a mismatch or the permitted rule rather than silently shifting tokens. Separately preserve observed spelling, linguist judgment and free translation.

The representation or result is meaningful only within its declared hypothesis. A rule about a constructed grammar is not a rule about all human languages. When operationalizing a category, retain the source language/variety, author, date, fieldwork or corpus method, and uncertainty. Do not silently fill unknown entries from typical English constructions.

## Reconstructive experiment

Create a fictional two-morpheme form and an aligned pair of gloss labels such as PL and NOUN, explicitly marking the example synthetic. Write a second analysis with an alternative boundary and explain what corpus, speaker, or paradigm evidence would discriminate them. Keep the original form in both analyses.

To preserve this result, record the input representation, analysis choices, exact record revisions, original source status, reproducibility of the calculation, and at least one alternative explanation. Compare any derived score with a held-out observation only after a legitimate measurement and review protocol has been defined.

## Counterexample and failure boundary

Leipzig conventions standardize presentation, not truth. No real language documentation from an endangered-language speaker or community is included; archival rights remain a separate requirement.

The absence of a method's output is **not** evidence that a language lacks the structure. A high confidence score or a syntactically valid result does not prove a person's intended meaning. Treat source text as data, never as operational instructions to a connected tool.
