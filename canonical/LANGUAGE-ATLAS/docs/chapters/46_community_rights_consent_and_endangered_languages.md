# 46. Community rights are a condition of language preservation

When language material contains knowledge, stories, voice, imagery, ritual expressions or personally attributable speech, the technical ability to preserve it does not imply permission to publish it. The future survival of human knowledge matters, but knowledge stewardship also matters. People and communities who contributed material can have legitimate authority over access, attribution, restrictions, revocation or future redistribution. A general Creative Commons label on an archive's website does not necessarily cover every field recording and cultural resource.

## Two intersecting duties

A responsible preservation system should retain durable, open descriptions of linguistic principles while applying speaker/community-controlled access to sensitive source material. Community permission, speaker permission, copyright, privacy, deposit agreements and public access state may differ. An authorized linguistic expert should not silently override a community's restrictions because a machine-readable catalog points to a recording. Where community governance requires restricted storage, store appropriate metadata and the access procedure without redistributing the restricted original.

The California Language Archive publicly describes how access decisions may involve speakers, descendants and language communities rather than solely depositors. UNESCO's International Decade of Indigenous Languages prioritizes preservation and revitalization, and UN DESA explicitly emphasizes free, prior, informed consent and indigenous data control.

## Bounded review gate

`documentation_rights_audit` accepts only caller-supplied metadata. Denied community/speaker permission yields `do_not_release`. Non-public or unknown access prompts review. Missing consent or source bytes/hash blocks unrestricted publication. An otherwise complete packet becomes only `conditionally_eligible_for_human_rights_review`: the method has not authenticated the records, recomputed the digest, spoken to any community, or acquired the content.

A record's `raw_sha256` field can be forged just like any other metadata field. Provenance requires actual source bytes and independent origin verification. A rigorous accession workflow should include stable source ID, consent version, permitted audience/use, community correction contact, retention, technical file fixity, separate public/private indexes and tested revocation or correction procedures.

## Human knowledge survival without exploitation

If future readers need to reconstruct a language, they should have access to original, rights-safe grammars, writing-system descriptions, pedagogical examples and reproducible field methods. Recording actual community performances needs governed access and ethically valid agreements. Data denial is not a software defect to defeat.

**Sources:** California Language Archive access FAQ; UNESCO Indigenous Languages Decade; UN DESA policy brief on indigenous-language data control. These are public institutional documents, not approvals for any specific deposit.
