# Investigation 37 — Counting languages does not provide independent trials

A toy typology table contains three languages in one family showing a binary feature and two in a second family with one known negative and one missing value. Known-item proportion is 3/4=.75; equal-family averaging gives (1+0)/2=.5. This arithmetic illustrates how sample composition changes descriptive statistics.

Neither statistic estimates the world's true language-wide distribution without a suitable sampling design, genealogy/area controls, missingness analysis and uncertainty. One family may contain closely related varieties, and source availability can itself bias the dataset. The correct output describes the chosen estimator and its denominator, not a universal linguistic law.
