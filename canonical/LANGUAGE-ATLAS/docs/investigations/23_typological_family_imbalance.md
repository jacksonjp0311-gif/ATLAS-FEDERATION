# Investigation 23: A language-family sample changes the estimated fraction

**Origin:** constructed offline study unless otherwise stated. No human participant or original media data was acquired.

Three constructed family A languages show a Boolean feature; family B has one observed absence and one unknown. The naive fraction among known rows is 3/4, but the equally weighted observed-family average is 1/2. Neither is a universal prevalence estimate. The example exposes sensitivity to what receives equal weight.

**Counterexample:** reclassify one language or add many related languages from A and the naive mean moves without any change in the underlying grammatical rule for the previously observed languages. Missing data can also be concentrated by family or geography, biasing both estimates.

**Next real study:** pin language identifiers, family taxonomy, area stratification, feature coding, sampling plan and source citations. WALS specifically cautions that genealogical and areal sampling affect global generalizations. None of its actual feature rows were imported in this experiment.

**Evidence status:** This is a reproducible bounded algorithm/method example. Human-language validity and independent review are not established.
