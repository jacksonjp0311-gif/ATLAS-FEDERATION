# Energy Atlas — rebuilding energy knowledge when networks are lost

**Only educational and planning information. No hazardous electrical, gas, fire, reactor, battery-construction, pressure-system or water-disinfection procedures are authorized by this reference.** Read with local emergency agencies and qualified engineering oversight wherever a real installation is contemplated.

## A recoverable learning sequence

**Stage 1: Reconstruct the unit system.** Establish the joule, watt, watt-hour, kilowatt-hour and Kelvin. Make a conversion table and test it in the pure-Python reference. Record measurement units, reference points, and which values come from experiments versus assumptions. The SI system defines some constants exactly; measured physical constants retain uncertainties.

**Stage 2: Learn conservation, heat and power limits.** Follow the first and second laws; understand that no passive device can deliver more useful energy than entered the defined boundary, while a heat pump's COP can exceed one by moving external heat. Derive an energy balance and identify every energy storage and loss path. Reproduce Carnot bounds and calculate sensible heating and shelter degree-hours as models only.

**Stage 3: Identify essential human services without making dangerous assumptions.** Document real demand data for water, food refrigeration, shelter and communications. Record the required **instantaneous power**, **daily energy**, starting current, duty cycle, voltage/frequency specification, permitted interruption and source of each requirement. Identify which services need external certified safety or clinical engineering; mathematical priority code is not such authority.

**Stage 4: Understand generation and storage as separate physical systems.** For each renewable source, document how energy conversion depends on resource, weather, equipment and efficiency. For batteries, distinguish stored kWh from deliverable kW, reserve state, charge/discharge loss and physical protection. DOE explains that ordinary grid-dependent solar cannot necessarily island through an outage; a legitimate islanded system needs appropriate inverters, storage and isolation/protection.

**Stage 5: Use energy-only ledgers to discover shortfalls.** Build 24-hour, seven-day and seasonal scenarios with explicitly sourced hourly or finer load and generation. Integrate supply, imports, curtailment, conversion losses, battery changes and unmet energy; verify residuals near zero. An energy accounting pass is not a grid-safety pass. Reconstruct all scenarios from the input time series rather than relying on charts.

**Stage 6: Protect the physical world.** For real generators, combustion, hot equipment, pressurized water, storage or electrical interfaces, stop at the educational boundary and consult current safety authorities and qualified operators. CDC warns against using fuel-burning generators indoors or near openings because of carbon monoxide. EPA warns that heating or disinfection cannot make chemically contaminated water safe merely by killing microorganisms. NFPA 855 is a copyrighted safety standard, not a free engineering procedure embedded in this library.

**Stage 7: Document source and calibration debt.** Every primary reference needs exact publisher version, rights, transport bytes where permitted, SHA-256, and an independently verified claim scope. Record what is not known. The current bibliography includes publisher URLs but not authenticated original publisher files, so future network loss would remove part of the independent evidence trail.

**Stage 8: Prove future improvements separately.** If a later energy design or Bitwork search algorithm is proposed to be more efficient, compare matched conditions, source-verified data, independent output measurements, and real cost. A 4,670-check synthetic oracle cannot prove actual reduced joules, outage tolerance, or equipment safety.
