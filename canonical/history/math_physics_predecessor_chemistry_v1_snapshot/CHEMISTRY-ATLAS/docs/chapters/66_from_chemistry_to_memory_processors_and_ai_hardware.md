## Scientific foundation

Modern computing hardware depends on chemical and physical foundations: semiconductor materials, dielectric films, metal interconnects, mechanical substrates, thermal interface materials, corrosion resistance, process purity and reliable measurements. These domains interact through energy conservation, defect statistics, reaction thermodynamics and transport. Knowledge survival requires preserving the theoretical foundations and validated materials properties even if a particular fabrication line becomes unavailable. This chapter constructs a dependency map rather than an industrial wafer-processing recipe or claims of a newly manufactured processor.

## Reconstruction and derivation

Chemistry supplies models of states and rates of reactions, materials supplies crystal/defect properties, electronics supplies physical circuits, computation supplies algorithms and data representation, and agent systems provide higher-level behavior. Each step demands independent evidence; the existence of a mathematically valid lower-level model does not guarantee a functioning transistor or chip. A future research program should make all dependencies machine-readable and validate interfaces with independent tests rather than repeatedly scraping text.
