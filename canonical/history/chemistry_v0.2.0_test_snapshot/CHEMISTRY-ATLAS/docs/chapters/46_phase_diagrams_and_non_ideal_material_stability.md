# 46. Phase diagrams and non-ideal material stability

**Field:** Materials · **Version:** v0.2.0 · **Scientific status:** original educational reference; cited publisher portions inspected, original publisher bytes not archived. No real experiment.

## Principles and physical scope

A material's stable phase depends on temperature, pressure, composition and, sometimes, history. Phase diagrams summarize equilibrium boundaries, but actual samples may contain metastable phases or kinetic barriers. An alloy or semiconductor with the correct element count can still have an unwanted microstructure that changes electronic and mechanical properties. Reconstructing useful materials science means preserving phase definitions, diagrams with legal rights, thermodynamic property sources, uncertainty and conditions, as well as the difference between equilibrium and observed production. A purely stoichiometric reaction calculation cannot tell whether a proposed solid phase exists under practical conditions.

## Equation and definitions

`For phases α and β at fixed T,p, equilibrium requires μ_i^α=μ_i^β for each transferring component i, under a shared reference/constraint model.`

Inputs and reference states must be declared. The algebra alone cannot establish source data validity, experimental behavior or physical safety.

## Derivation and why the relationship holds

The Gibbs free energy of a multicomponent system is minimized at equilibrium subject to mass conservation. Equal chemical potentials across coexisting phases follow from considering an infinitesimal transfer at constant total amounts and requiring the first-order Gibbs change to vanish. Real kinetics and nucleation can trap metastable states. A phase boundary read from a diagram must preserve its axes, units, uncertainty and underlying data source; mere curve reproduction is not an independent experimental measurement.

## Reconstruct without external AI

Consider two fictional phases where the stated chemical potential difference changes sign with temperature. At the crossing, the idealized equilibrium preference can change; write the sign-dependent inference without proposing a manufacturing process. Identify why crystal defects, strain and kinetic arrest might prevent immediate observation of the predicted phase.

These examples are invented, not laboratory readings. Reproduce them by hand and compare only within the declared model domain.

## Failure case and missing evidence

Phase data are not universal material properties independent of reference state. Temperature-ramp protocols, pressure vessels and precursor syntheses need qualified safety and source evidence; none are provided.
