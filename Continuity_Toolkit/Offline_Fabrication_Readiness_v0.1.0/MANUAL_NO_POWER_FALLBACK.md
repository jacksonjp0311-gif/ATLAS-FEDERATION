# No printer power? The knowledge should still help.

The designed part is a small *former for winding a noncritical demonstration coil*. If a printer is unavailable, a separate low-tech educational approximation can be made from a **nonconductive cardboard or wooden tube** with two fixed cardboard/wood discs serving as end flanges. This is an instructional alternative, **not** an interchangeable certified replacement or guaranteed dimensional duplicate.

Measure the tube and central bore with a ruler or calipers. Keep the assembly dry and smooth enough that it does not damage wire insulation. Secure the flanges by a method appropriate to the available materials. Keep ends and fastenings away from moving parts. It may be used as a spool or a low-power hand-winding aid, but it is **not** suitable for high voltage, mains transformers, high-current inductors, pressure components, load-bearing machinery, food/water equipment or temperature-critical uses without an appropriate qualified design and independent testing.

Without electric power the 3D printer is not useful. Preserve the Atlas books and offline field guide in print, and focus effort on safe heat, drinking water from verified sources, shelter and communications before experimental fabrication.
