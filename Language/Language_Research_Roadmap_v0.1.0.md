# Language Atlas — Route from initial reference to scoped v1

1. **Representation and normative tests:** obtain version-matched, rights-cleared Unicode UAX 29 and UAX 15 test data; compare fully conformant grapheme, word, sentence and normalization behavior across scripts; report Unicode data version rather than assuming Python supports the newest standard.
2. **Real linguistic evidence:** negotiate rights and community access for genuinely licensed multi-script and multilingual corpora; preserve original bytes, annotation conventions, dialect labels and privacy status. Begin with public WALS/UD/CLDF resources where permitted, distinguishing source licensing and each treebank's rights.
3. **Independent annotation:** ask qualified speakers/linguists to evaluate at least representative morphology, syntax, semantics, spoken/signed language examples. Account for disagreements, cultural ownership and alternative analyses.
4. **Deeper science:** finite-state transducers and feature unification; parsing beyond CNF, phonological feature geometry, syntax/semantic scope, acoustic feature extraction with real calibration, and historical comparative methods.
5. **Evaluation:** properly split datasets by source/speaker/time, test against independent baselines with robust unknown labels, retain errors and negative outcomes. Do not use the Atlas's own teaching exercises as a held-out benchmark after training on them.
6. **Survival acceptance:** original rights-cleared primary-source bytes with attribution/verified hashes, at least one offline restore, full human reconstruction instructions, review by linguistic and community experts, and clear unresolved-source list.

Keep common routing unchanged. Complete all 30 domain content references before building Bitwork routing, then integrate and transfer to desktop.
