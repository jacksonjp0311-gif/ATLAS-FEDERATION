# Chapter 01. What a human language is and what a language model is not

**Area:** Foundations  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Human language is a community-shared, learned system for expressing meanings, coordinating action, maintaining relationships, and transmitting culture. Linguists study its sound or sign form, grammatical patterns, semantics, use in interaction, acquisition, change, and social variation. A language is not reducible to a spelling system, a dictionary or a statistical model trained on texts. Spoken and signed languages organize linguistic form through different physical modalities yet both possess systematic combinations of meaningful units. The existence of a rule is an empirical claim about users and contexts, not a licensing decision by a machine parser. A reference to the language of a person or community requires self-identification, appropriate historical perspective, and evidence from its users.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

Distinguish the representation levels: acoustic/visual gesture, phonetic or sign units, phonological contrasts, morphemes, phrases, semantic interpretations, discourse moves, and the social situation. Mapping between them is many-to-many rather than an invertible chain. Different messages can express the same proposition with different pragmatic force; the same sentence can be interpreted differently across situations. A language model estimates properties of a supplied distribution of strings or other representations. Statistical predictability does not imply the speaker's intended meaning, truthful content, human-like understanding or full sociolinguistic competence.

# Chapter 02. Speech articulation and the production of linguistic sound

**Area:** Phonetics  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Speech uses airflow, voicing, and articulatory gestures by the respiratory system, larynx, tongue, jaw, palate and lips. Consonants can be classified by place and manner of articulation and voicing; vowels are described by tongue position, openness, lip rounding and other features. These labels are abstractions over continuous coarticulation, not fixed physical coordinates. Speech sound production also depends on individual anatomy, dialect, phonological context, health, and speaking style. Written spellings are not reliable phonetic recordings: a letter may stand for different phones or none, while one phone can have multiple orthographic representations.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A useful abstract phonetic token can be represented as a vector of independently declared features such as [voice], place and manner. A Hamming distance between two such vectors counts stipulated categorical differences, not physical acoustic dissimilarity or perceptual confusion. Some phonetic differences are continuous (formant trajectories, duration, fundamental frequency), and others involve timing and overlapping gestures. For a real measurement, record the microphone, sampling rate, environment and annotator; an IPA symbol is a transcription decision, not an instrument reading.

# Chapter 03. Acoustic phonetics, speech signals and uncertainty

**Area:** Phonetics  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Acoustic speech consists of time-varying pressure changes, usually measured by a transducer and digitized. Relevant analyses may include time-domain waveforms, spectrograms, spectral envelope, formants, pitch-related periodicity, duration, loudness and noise. No single number fully describes a spoken vowel or consonant. A physical fundamental-frequency estimate depends on the presence of periodic voicing and on the estimator and window; a child, an adult and a speaker with irregular phonation may produce very different signals. Coarticulation and environmental noise limit simplistic phone segmentation.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

Sampling converts a continuous signal to a discrete series x[n] at sample rate f_s. For a truly band-limited input and ideal sampling, the Nyquist criterion requires f_s greater than twice the highest signal frequency for unaliased reconstruction. This does not imply speech itself is perfectly band-limited or that a poor microphone records every relevant articulation. The discrete Fourier transform summarizes a finite window's frequency content; time-frequency resolution trades off with window length. A spectrogram's apparent patterns must be tied to a disclosed window, normalization and color scale.

# Chapter 04. Signed languages, gesture, and visual linguistic form

**Area:** Modalities  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Signed languages are naturally occurring languages expressed through manual and nonmanual articulators. Handshape, location, movement, orientation, facial expression and bodily timing can contribute to linguistic contrasts and grammatical structure. Signed languages are not merely word-by-word renderings of the surrounding spoken language. Their grammars, discourse and sociolinguistic histories deserve primary attention, including Deaf communities' expertise and leadership. Gesture and conventional sign are not identical categories, and a mixed multimodal interaction may include both. A linguistic archive that collects only orthographic text has failed to preserve much of the communicative system.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A visual utterance can be represented as multiple time-aligned tracks. A symbolic gloss may help analysts communicate, but it loses kinematic and interactional detail. A video annotation requires time intervals, informed permission and a separation between observable gestures, analyst-inferred linguistic features and community translations. Nonmanual signals can overlap manual signs, so a forced one-token-at-a-time linear text model is not a complete representation. Meaning is constructed through syntax, discourse, context and agreed conventions, not a universal hand-gesture dictionary.

# Chapter 05. Phonemes, phones, and allophonic distribution

**Area:** Phonology  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

A phone is an analytically recognized speech sound; a phoneme is a unit of contrast within a particular language or variety. A minimal pair can support phonemic status when two forms differ in a targeted sound and differ in meaning, but uncontrolled spelling, tone, stress or morphological distinctions can confound the inference. Allophones are contextually distributed realizations of a phonemic category under a particular analysis. Contrastive and complementary distributions are useful evidence, not infallible algorithms that decide linguistic structure from a small word list.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A feature matrix abstracts possible oppositions. In a constructed system, /p/ and /b/ may differ by one voicing dimension, while [n] and [ŋ] may be linked by an assimilation rule before a velar consonant. Both analyses need corpus support and speaker judgment before being claimed for a real language. A phonological rule is a mapping conditioned by environment and grammar; a phonetic articulatory tendency can motivate it without making the rule universal. Rule ordering and representation choices can yield different apparent analyses.

**Area:** Phonology  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Speech communication is structured above the segment level. Stress can organize prominence in a word or phrase; lexical tone can distinguish words or grammatical categories; intonation patterns can help organize information, stance or discourse; duration and rhythm can carry contrasts. The boundaries between stress, tone, accent and intonation are language-specific and contested in individual analyses. A rising F0 contour does not universally signal a question, nor does falling F0 prove certainty. The same prosodic gesture can depend on voice quality, speaking rate and social convention.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A time-aligned prosodic annotation should distinguish observed physical contours from phonological categories and inferred social meaning. Relative timing, syllable boundaries and normalized pitch may be more interpretable than raw F0 in hertz across speakers. In a tonal language, tone-bearing units and morphological context must be specified. Information theory can quantify distributions of contours only after a stable measurement and label protocol; the numerical entropy itself cannot identify pragmatic meaning.

# Chapter 07. Phonotactics, syllables, and sound-pattern restrictions

**Area:** Phonology  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Phonotactics concerns restrictions and preferences on combinations of sounds in a language. Syllables organize segments into structures that may be described with onsets, nuclei and codas; phonological weight, sonority and stress can interact with syllable parsing. A sequence impossible in one stipulated toy grammar can be common in another language, so rejection by a parser does not mean the sequence is inherently unpronounceable. Loanwords, rapid speech, morphological boundaries and dialect variation complicate simple restrictions. Phonotactic generalizations should be supported by representative forms and speakers, not only by a restricted list of spellings.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A finite-state acceptor can encode permitted syllable shapes (for example a stipulated CV or CVC system). It can test membership efficiently but only relative to its declared alphabet and transition rules. Productive phonological patterning may be gradient, stochastic, feature-based or conditioned by morphological structure. A finite acceptor is not a theory of every speaker's competence; the model's outputs should be interpreted as “licensed by this grammar” and “not licensed by this grammar.”

# Chapter 08. Morphology: words, lexemes, morphemes and segmentation

**Area:** Morphology  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

Morphology studies how word forms relate to lexical meanings and grammatical functions. A lexeme is an abstract lexical unit, while a word form is a particular realization; a morpheme is a theoretical minimal meaningful unit whose surface realization may be conditioned by context. Segmentation can be ambiguous: an apparently meaningful substring may be coincidental, and a lexical item may not admit a clean concatenative analysis. Analysts must distinguish inflectional features, derivational relationships, clitics and compounds and must not assume that the English space-separated “word” is a universal linguistic unit.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

A finite lexicon can map a surface string to multiple analyses with lemma, features and evidence provenance. It may return “unknown” when a form is missing, but that result means the lexicon is incomplete, not that the word is impossible. Finite-state transducers can encode regular relations between underlying and surface strings; this does not guarantee that every language's morphology is regular or concatenative. Lexicon source IDs and language variety must remain attached to the output.

# Chapter 33. N-gram language models, smoothing and perplexity

**Area:** Computation  
**Content maturity:** original educational reconstruction. No independent scientific or community review.  
**Primary-source access:** source links and scoped summaries only; original publisher bytes are not present.

## Foundations and definitions

An n-gram model estimates the probability of a token from a bounded history of preceding tokens. Bigram counts are easy to compute and inspect, making them valuable for teaching conditional probability and sparsity. They do not capture arbitrary long-distance syntax, conversation intention or world knowledge. Unknown tokens and sentence-start/end markers must be handled explicitly. With unsmoothed estimates, many perfectly ordinary unseen sequences receive zero probability; add-k smoothing avoids that but imposes a modeling choice that can distort distributions. Evaluation must use a genuinely held-out corpus and a fixed tokenization policy.

## Underlying mathematical, grammatical or methodological principle

For a conditional bigram estimate, P(w|h)=(count(h,w)+α)/(count(h)+α|V|) with α>0 and prediction vocabulary V. Sentence log-probability is the sum of log probabilities for each transition; perplexity PP=2^{−(1/N)Σ log₂P(w_i|h_i)}. Perplexity is sensitive to tokenization and unknown-token handling; scores from incomparable vocabularies or corpora cannot be directly interpreted as understanding. Low perplexity can result from memorization or repeated strings.

# 39. Cross-language comparison requires a sampling design

Language is universal to humans, but linguistic properties vary substantially across families, regions, histories, communities, modalities, and traditions of documentation. A claim about “most languages” needs an explicit sampling population and a consistent definition of the measured feature. Counting the number of entries returned by a database is insufficient. Some language families contain thousands of documented varieties, while others have a single listed language; their representation in a dataset reflects scholarly attention as well as genealogical realities.

## What is counted

Define a unit of analysis: a named language, a dialect, a speaker, a construction, a recorded utterance, or a language-family lineage. A word-order construction can depend on clause type and information structure; labeling one language simply SOV or SVO can hide internal variability. Language identifiers must be tied to a named classification release and a source; a label alone does not guarantee a consistent population over time.

WALS's introduction explains why maximizing genealogical and areal diversity is crucial and documents the composition and documentation gaps of its samples. The observed fraction of languages possessing feature F is `p_naive=(Σ_i 1[F_i])/(number of known F_i)`. A simple sensitivity statistic is `p_family=(1/K)Σ_f ((Σ_{i in f}1[F_i])/(number of known F_i in family f))`, where K is the number of families with at least one observed value. These quantities answer different descriptive questions. Equal family weighting does not remove areal diffusion, inconsistent family taxonomy, missingness, or nonrandom language selection.

## Demonstration and counterexample

Consider a toy sample: three languages in family A all show F, while one of two languages in family B lacks F and the other has an unknown value. The naive observed fraction is 3/4; equal family weighting produces (1+0)/2=1/2. Neither proportion estimates the worldwide frequency without a defensible sample and missingness model. A database's “unknown” must not automatically become a false feature observation.

`typology_by_family` computes both figures, records unknown values and source-supplied family assignments, and refuses duplicate language IDs. It does not authenticate those assignments. Genuine scientific work should stratify by genealogical and geographic factors, document feature criteria, examine family reclassifications and estimate uncertainty with clustering or appropriate models rather than treating all languages as independent random draws.

## What a future reader must preserve

Record survey design, language IDs, feature definitions, source passages and licensing, sampled communities, classification versions, exclusion criteria, unknown/mixed cases, and data transformed from raw observations. Preserve the raw feature judgments separately from aggregate statistics. A queryable database without these facts cannot support reproducible linguistic universals.

**Sources:** WALS introduction (Comrie, Dryer, Gil and Haspelmath, publisher page) and CLDF specification; these are selected documentation pages, not original sampled data acquired in this release.

# 40. BLEU evaluates token overlap under explicit reference conditions

Machine translation quality is not a single number. Adequacy, factual fidelity, terminology, register, politeness, cultural meaning, preservation of negation, and usability to real speakers can diverge even when token overlap looks strong. BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002) compares system output n-grams with one or more human reference translations and applies a penalty for very short outputs. It is primarily a **corpus-level** statistic under specified tokenization, reference composition, and smoothing, not a semantic correctness oracle for one sentence.

## Mathematical contract

For order n, count each candidate n-gram's occurrences and clip them to the maximum occurrences of that n-gram in any reference for the corresponding sentence. Accumulate clipped and candidate counts over all sentences to get precision `p_n`. Let c be the total candidate-token length and r the sum of closest reference lengths (tie convention declared). The brevity penalty is `BP=1` for c≥r and `BP=exp(1−r/c)` for 0<c<r. A simple unsmoothed BLEU score of order N is `BP * exp((1/N)Σ_n log(p_n))`, defined as zero when any required p_n is zero. The function has no meaning independent of the tokenizer's unit.

The original 2002 paper introduced a useful automatic evaluation framework. Post's 2018 SacreBLEU paper emphasizes that normalizing and tokenizing data differently makes published scores incomparable. Our `corpus_bleu` is explicitly **not SacreBLEU** and does not implement the WMT signature, reference normalization, or its smoothing options. It only demonstrates the transparent arithmetic on caller-supplied token arrays.

## A falsifying case

A candidate translation containing the exact reference tokens may score 1.0 even if the reference translation itself is culturally inappropriate or false. Conversely, a valid paraphrase can have low n-gram overlap. A one-word candidate may have a nonzero unigram match but a zero four-gram precision under unsmoothed BLEU, and therefore BLEU4=0. None of these outcomes licenses a claim about a model's actual understanding.

## How to reconstruct

Write each reference token sequence, enumerate the candidate's n-grams, apply the per-sentence clipped maximum, aggregate counts, compute closest-reference corpus length, and then report the exact options: tokenization, case treatment, Unicode normalization, references, max order, smoothing, and metric implementation/version. For a serious system comparison, include human judgments on an independently selected sample, preserve error categories such as missing negation or term mistranslation, and avoid training/test leakage.

**Sources:** Papineni et al., *BLEU* (ACL 2002); Matt Post, *A Call for Clarity in Reporting BLEU Scores* (WMT 2018). Only source metadata and scoped explanations are retained; no original paper bytes were mirrored.

# 41. Alignment error rates depend on segmentation and evidence

Speech recognition, transcription and historical comparison frequently align two sequences. A sequence is not necessarily a sequence of English words: it may be graphemes, phonemes, morphemes, gloss tokens or segments defined by an annotation project. The unit determines both interpretation and error counts. Standard edit distance minimizes substitutions, insertions and deletions under fixed costs; one alignment may be optimal among multiple alternatives.

## Dynamic programming

For reference tokens `r_1…r_m` and hypothesis tokens `h_1…h_n`, define `D(i,0)=i` and `D(0,j)=j`. Recurrence: `D(i,j)=min(D(i−1,j)+1, D(i,j−1)+1, D(i−1,j−1)+[r_i≠h_j])`. A reverse traceback produces one optimal path and records matches, substitutions, deletions and insertions. Under the usual definition, word error rate is `(S+D+I)/N_ref`, where N_ref is the number of reference words; with N_ref=0, the normalized rate is undefined, not zero. WER can exceed one when insertions dominate.

`aligned_edit_distance` implements this teaching calculation with an explicit finite grid cap and returns one witness. It neither detects the speaker's intended words nor authenticates the reference. Text normalization, language-specific word boundaries, transcription conventions and punctuation affect all results. A signed-language movement sequence or simultaneous non-manual feature tier is not faithfully represented by one token stream.

## Example

For reference `['i','like','cats']` and hypothesis `['i','adore','cats']`, one substitution yields distance 1 and error rate 1/3. The result says nothing about whether “adore” conveys acceptable meaning in the context. Two equally valid transcripts may differ in word segmentation and appear to have errors even when the sound signal is similar. A linguistic atlas must preserve the transcription scheme and source audio/video rights where permitted.

## Evidence requirements

A real ASR or documentation evaluation should preserve the source recording and permitted access, time alignment, speaker/dialect context, reference annotators, disagreements, normalization rules, and boundaries used by the metric. Errors from small datasets or unrepresentative domains cannot be scaled to all speakers. A low token error rate can hide catastrophic errors on names, medication instructions, negation or consent conditions.

**Sources:** Jurafsky and Martin *Speech and Language Processing* online draft (selected publisher overview); Unicode UAX #29; existing Atlas segmentation and morphology references. No actual audio/transcription dataset was imported.

# 42. Probabilistic grammars and the difference between likelihood and truth

A context-free grammar lists derivational possibilities. A probabilistic context-free grammar (PCFG) assigns a probability to each production, usually normalized over alternative productions with the same left-hand nonterminal. The grammar defines a generative distribution over derivations, not a universal statement about what speakers may say. A sentence may have multiple derivations with the same surface words, making ambiguity integral to the task.

## Inside recurrence

For a strict Chomsky-normal-form grammar with lexical A→word and binary A→B C productions, let `I(A,i,j)` be the total probability of A deriving token span [i,j). For a one-token span, I sums lexical rule weights with that token. For longer spans, `I(A,i,j)=Σ_{A→B C} p(A→B C)Σ_{k=i+1}^{j−1}I(B,i,k)I(C,k,j)`. Summing over rule choices and split locations preserves multiple parses rather than arbitrarily choosing one. The Viterbi recurrence instead replaces sums over alternatives with a maximum and returns one highest-probability derivation witness.

An unsmoothed parser can underflow in floating point when multiplying very many small probabilities. Production probability estimates from sparse corpora can be biased, and grammar coverage may be poor for idioms, non-concatenative morphology and spontaneous speech. The present `pcfg_inside` restricts inputs to small bounded CNF grammars and valid normalized rule distributions; it does not fit parameters or parse arbitrary human language.

## Constructed demonstration

Create lexical rules N→birds and V→sing, each probability 1, and S→N V probability 1. The two-token sentence has inside probability 1 under this tiny grammar. Replace `sing` with an unlisted token: the probability becomes 0 under the supplied grammar. This means **unlicensed by the grammar**, not that the natural-language sentence is inherently ungrammatical. Add two alternative S derivations to the same surface; the inside probability sums both, while the best derivation retains only the maximum. A model that reports only the best tree loses the ambiguity mass.

## Reconstruct by hand

List all rules and probabilities grouped by left-hand category, confirm row sums, build the triangle of spans of length 1 through n, enumerate every split, and compare inside and Viterbi values. In a multilingual corpus, evaluate grammar coverage and held-out likelihood separately from syntactic adequacy, and inspect examples where alternative analyses remain linguistically reasonable.

**Sources:** Chomsky (1956) on formal grammar classes; Jurafsky and Martin's online language-processing draft for probabilistic grammars. Publisher texts are linked, not copied.

# 43. Historical sound correspondence: recurrence is evidence, not proof by resemblance

Comparative historical linguistics investigates relationships among languages through systematic sound correspondences, shared innovations, morphology, chronology, and alternative explanations such as borrowing, chance similarity and contact. Similar-looking words do not prove common origin. A table of aligned segment pairs can be an exploratory observation, but it relies on a prior decision about word comparability and alignment. Those judgments must be recorded separately from any inference about language families.

## The constructed alignment model

`aligned_sound_correspondences` accepts stipulated aligned segment sequences for named language IDs, original source IDs and candidate word/item IDs. It counts pairwise segment correspondences except gap columns, rejecting alignment-length mismatches and duplicate item identifiers. It reports language pairs and counts. It never assigns sound-change probabilities, declares cognacy, infers ancestral segments, or constructs a phylogenetic tree.

For toy words /pat/ and /fat/, aligned position counts are p↔f, a↔a, t↔t. That observation alone is insufficient to prove a regular change p→f. The same correspondence should be supported by independently sourced candidate cognate sets, controlled for chronology, lexical borrowing and chance. A relationship of a sound correspondence to a phonological system may vary by environment and grammatical condition. A zero count in an incomplete sample is not evidence of impossibility.

## Reconstruction procedure

Preserve the original orthographic form and source recording or transcription scheme. Normalize IPA only with an explicitly defined mapping; never silently discard diacritics or tone. Build a candidate cognate set, record hypotheses and reasons for accepting or rejecting each alignment, compare recurring correspondences across multiple lexical items and grammatical environments, examine borrowing and geographic contact, and test whether a proposed sound-change system predicts unseen forms.

A robust corpus needs provenance for each source and language classification version. The content should distinguish field observation, transcription, analytical alignment and reconstructed historical claim. The model must permit disagreement and uncertainty rather than synthesizing a confident ancestral form from insufficient data.

**Sources:** Glottolog bibliography and WALS linguistic classification for context, with existing phonetics/phonology chapters. This version implements correspondence counting only, not a scientific reconstruction of any real language.

# 44. Genealogical and geographic dependence in language typology

Cross-linguistic samples violate naïve statistical independence in several ways. Language varieties share inherited properties through common ancestry, borrow innovations through contact, and respond to social/ecological contexts. Databases overrepresent languages with extensive scholarship and may encode categories differently between studies. An observation count is therefore neither a random sample of all human languages nor a direct statement of a universal probability.

The World Atlas of Language Structures introduction explicitly considers genealogical and areal diversity when selecting reference samples. It notes missing documentation and differing representation among families. Any feature survey should preserve the definition of each variable, original judgments, source pages, language IDs and genealogical classification revision. Missingness should be kept distinct from absence.

## Two descriptive estimands

A naïve known-value fraction weighs each observed language once. An equal-family fraction weighs each represented family's internal known-value fraction once. More sophisticated estimators may account for phylogenetic trees, contact networks, geography, measurement error and unequal sampling weights. None can be inferred merely from a database's family names. The two simple fractions serve a **sensitivity check**: if they differ markedly, assumptions about which unit receives equal weight are consequential.

`typology_by_family` returns both values and preserves unknown feature counts. It validates one row per language ID and Boolean/unknown feature values. Its family labels are caller-supplied, not verified phylogeny. Equal weighting can itself distort a population estimand when family classification is disputed or many families lack data. Areal correlation is not removed by equal family weighting.

## Case-based explanation

If three related languages show a feature and one unrelated language does not, an observed 3/4 rate can become a 1/2 equal-family rate. If the missing values are more common in one family, both numbers can be biased. A future analyst should state which population is being estimated and why a particular sampling scheme provides warranted inference.

**Sources:** WALS Introduction by Comrie, Dryer, Gil and Haspelmath; CLDF source/provenance conventions. Both are scoped publicly available publisher references; this release includes no original typological dataset rows.

# 45. Cross-Linguistic Data Formats and reproducible scholarly evidence

An archive of linguistic measurements must be more than a spreadsheet. The Cross-Linguistic Data Formats (CLDF) specification builds on the W3C CSV on the Web model and uses UTF-8 CSV data with a JSON metadata description. It supplies conventional data modules and field meanings, identifiers, foreign-key relationships, sources and standards for null values. A reproducible human-language knowledge base needs a similar separation between observed claims, their sources, the analysis performed, and the derived quantitative results.

## Why column metadata matters

A column labeled `Language` may contain names, codes or local IDs; `Form` may contain orthography or IPA tokens; `Value` can be a coded observation or missing. Without precise field semantics, future readers may conflate no data with the linguistic absence of a feature. CLDF's documented missingness model distinguishes a row not extracted from source from a row whose source explicitly marks a value as null. Both can require different analysis.

A source reference should point to a stable bibliographic record, with page or context where known. Data transformations need exact code, version and parameters. If a researcher normalizes Unicode strings, removes punctuation, combines dialects or maps IPA symbols, the resulting dataset must not be represented as raw observation. The original file bytes, if lawfully retained, need independent hashes, source rights and a transformation lineage.

## Reproduction path

For a single fictitious language-feature dataset, create `languages.csv` with stable IDs, `features.csv` with explicitly nullable values, `sources.bib`, and metadata describing table/field relationships. Check referential integrity and source identifiers before aggregating. Record which table records are user-created examples versus observed community data. For a real dataset, do not assume that the CLDF schema implies legal rights to redistribute recordings or texts.

The current Atlas uses its own common JSONL contract; it does **not** claim full CLDF 1.3 conformance or a complete CLDF importer. This chapter teaches how an independently understandable source format supports survival beyond a particular database or web server.

**Source:** Official CLDF specification (public GitHub and CLDF project site), selected conformance and source-provenance sections. The exact specification bytes have not been archived locally.

# 46. Community rights are a condition of language preservation

When language material contains knowledge, stories, voice, imagery, ritual expressions or personally attributable speech, the technical ability to preserve it does not imply permission to publish it. The future survival of human knowledge matters, but knowledge stewardship also matters. People and communities who contributed material can have legitimate authority over access, attribution, restrictions, revocation or future redistribution. A general Creative Commons label on an archive's website does not necessarily cover every field recording and cultural resource.

## Two intersecting duties

A responsible preservation system should retain durable, open descriptions of linguistic principles while applying speaker/community-controlled access to sensitive source material. Community permission, speaker permission, copyright, privacy, deposit agreements and public access state may differ. An authorized linguistic expert should not silently override a community's restrictions because a machine-readable catalog points to a recording. Where community governance requires restricted storage, store appropriate metadata and the access procedure without redistributing the restricted original.

The California Language Archive publicly describes how access decisions may involve speakers, descendants and language communities rather than solely depositors. UNESCO's International Decade of Indigenous Languages prioritizes preservation and revitalization, and UN DESA explicitly emphasizes free, prior, informed consent and indigenous data control.

## Bounded review gate

`documentation_rights_audit` accepts only caller-supplied metadata. Denied community/speaker permission yields `do_not_release`. Non-public or unknown access prompts review. Missing consent or source bytes/hash blocks unrestricted publication. An otherwise complete packet becomes only `conditionally_eligible_for_human_rights_review`: the method has not authenticated the records, recomputed the digest, spoken to any community, or acquired the content.

A record's `raw_sha256` field can be forged just like any other metadata field. Provenance requires actual source bytes and independent origin verification. A rigorous accession workflow should include stable source ID, consent version, permitted audience/use, community correction contact, retention, technical file fixity, separate public/private indexes and tested revocation or correction procedures.

## Human knowledge survival without exploitation

If future readers need to reconstruct a language, they should have access to original, rights-safe grammars, writing-system descriptions, pedagogical examples and reproducible field methods. Recording actual community performances needs governed access and ethically valid agreements. Data denial is not a software defect to defeat.

**Sources:** California Language Archive access FAQ; UNESCO Indigenous Languages Decade; UN DESA policy brief on indigenous-language data control. These are public institutional documents, not approvals for any specific deposit.

# 47. Signed languages require simultaneous, not merely sequential, representation

Signed languages are natural languages with multiple interacting articulatory channels. Manual movement, handshape, location, orientation, facial expression, torso and gaze can contribute linguistic structure. A transcript rendered as one line of English glosses loses simultaneity, spatial relationships and many grammatical features. Automatic text-only transformation cannot be assumed to preserve their meaning. The distinction between a gesture and a signed linguistic element is language- and context-dependent and requires expertise and source observation.

## An annotation model

A practical annotation package uses tiers: each tier names one observation channel, time intervals have a declared time base, and annotations include source identity and analysis revision. Two tiers may overlap legitimately; for example, a facial expression can co-occur with a manual sign, and nonmanual markers may span multiple sequential manual events. Even within one tier overlap can reflect alternative analyses, not necessarily a data error.

`multimodal_tier_audit` checks bounded metadata, finite nonnegative start and end times, unique event IDs and reports within-tier overlap without declaring the transcript invalid. It does not process actual video or recognize a sign. A source video that is not stored cannot be reconstructed from a timing table; the Atlas must explicitly mark the missing media. Audio/video, transcript, phonetic descriptions and morphological glosses are distinct layers of evidence.

## Reconstruction requirements

For real language data, preservation would require source recording rights and consent, frame rate and synchronization, named annotation tiers, video quality, filming conditions, community-preferred transcription, gloss vocabulary, analysis disagreements, event IDs and ability to connect the original media to its annotations. None of those materials is acquired here. The toy case has a hands tier from 0–250 ms and a face tier from 100–200 ms; the overlap is allowed and does not demonstrate any particular grammatical meaning.

**Source:** Linguistic Society of America's public signed-language reference, UD/glossing context and ethical documentation references. No authentic signed-language recording or signer evaluation is included.

# 48. Unicode 18 and the requirement to version text segmentation

Text systems often count code points but present grapheme clusters to users. The Unicode Consortium's Standard Annex #29 defines default grapheme, word and sentence boundaries, and its rules change as the standard evolves. A single displayed grapheme may contain multiple scalar values, such as a letter plus a combining mark, an emoji sequence using a joiner, or a regional-indicator flag sequence. Counting code points with `len(text)` is not equivalent to counting perceived characters or word boundaries.

## Version evidence

The published UAX #29 revision 49, associated with Unicode 18.0.0 (1 September 2026), describes legacy and extended grapheme clusters and includes rules governing Indic conjunct linking, emoji joiners and regional indicators. The exact Unicode property databases and rule versions matter. Python's `unicodedata` carries its own version, which may lag the latest Unicode Standard. A language archive must record both the text's original Unicode code points and the segmentation implementation/version used to derive boundaries.

Neither the original v0.1 `unicode_profile` nor the new v0.2 methods implement normative UAX 29 extended grapheme segmentation. The profile deliberately returns `grapheme_cluster_count=null` instead of pretending that code-point count is correct. That refusal remains important for accessibility, UI cursor motion, text comparison and cross-script evaluation.

## Reconstruct without data loss

Keep original source bytes, encoding, normalization form, segmentation library/version, and token boundary offsets. Normalization forms NFC and NFKC differ: compatibility normalization can deliberately collapse distinct glyph semantics. If normalized strings are used to detect benchmark leakage, preserve the raw-to-normalized transformation and do not claim that exact string comparison detects all paraphrases or cross-language translations.

A future implementation should use a properly versioned Unicode property database and a conformance test suite, not a handmade approximation of emoji/Indic rules. Only then can the archive claim UAX 29 conformity for a specific version. Code that works on English text may silently fail on Thai, Khmer, Devanagari or emoji clusters.

**Source:** Unicode UAX #29 revision 49, v18.0 (2026-09-01) and the Unicode Standard; inspected published overview and boundary-rule sections, not original test-vector bytes.

# 49. Predictive metrics are not communicative intelligence

Probabilistic models assign likelihoods to sequences under a chosen alphabet and tokenization. Perplexity is the exponent of average negative log likelihood per token. It is useful for comparing models under identical data and token conventions, but it is not calibrated factual accuracy or a measure of human understanding. Two language models with different tokenizers may report different perplexity values even if their generated content is similarly useful. Their training corpora may overlap with evaluation texts, making memorization appear to be generalization.

## Units and estimands

A token-level perplexity `PP=exp(-(1/N)Σ log p(token_i|history))` depends on the tokenization unit and the test distribution. Character, subword, morpheme and word perplexity are different quantities. In a strongly inflected language, words may have many surface variants, so naïve cross-language comparisons of word-level perplexity are misleading. Evaluation must report unknown tokens, smoothing, calibration, model version and held-out split provenance.

BLEU and edit distance measure surface agreement with references, not semantic truth. A high BLEU score can coexist with a serious negation error; low BLEU may reflect a valid synonym or idiomatic translation. A grammar parser can reject a natural utterance absent from its tiny lexicon. A morphological analyzer can return two analyses for one form without deciding which a speaker intended. These are expected limits of the operational models, not software errors to conceal.

## Experimental design

To demonstrate that Language Atlas improves AI communication, use unseen tasks that test grammaticality, factual meaning, pragmatic appropriateness, multilingual translation, dialect sensitivity and signed-language accessibility. Include native speakers, trained annotators and transparent consent where relevant. Avoid one numerical score collapsing systematic failures on rare languages into good performance on high-resource English.

The Atlas itself is an evidence reference. It does not train a language model, call a provider, or claim a downstream model gain. Its purpose is to make future models and people more capable of reasoning about language with correct qualifications and reproducible methods.

**Sources:** Jurafsky/Martin language-model overview; Papineni BLEU; Post SacreBLEU reporting cautions; Unicode text-processing standards. No real multilingual evaluation run in this edition.

# Investigation 22: Inside mass versus maximum derivation

**Origin:** constructed offline study unless otherwise stated. No human participant or original media data was acquired.

A toy grammar assigns N→birds and V→sing probability one and S→N V probability one. Its inside probability for “birds sing” is exactly one. Replace “sing” with an unlisted word and the grammar assigns probability zero. That means it is *unlicensed by this tiny grammar*, not impossible in a human language.

**Ambiguity test:** two alternative binary derivations of the same two-token sentence receive weights p and 1−p. Inside probability sums to one, while the single highest-probability derivation has probability max(p,1−p). Returning one tree without total mass conceals competing analyses.

**Limit:** this code handles only small CNF grammars, normalized per nonterminal, no epsilon/unary rules or learned probabilities. True syntax, semantics and speaker acceptance require independent data.

**Maturity gate:** maintain separate flags for content completeness, authentic-source bytes, independent scholarly review, native-speaker/community validation, model evaluation, and live deployment. A clean code test suite must not force those flags true.

Language Atlas still needs deeper research before scoped v1, especially authentic language data and separate rights-cleared review. The other eight nodes remain frozen, and the final Bitwork router is deferred until all 30 domain libraries exist.

**Evidence status:** This is a reproducible bounded algorithm/method example. Human-language validity and independent review are not established.

## Human Knowledge Survival Charter
# ATLAS FORGE — Human Knowledge Survival Charter

**Edition 1.0 · 13 November 2026 · Foundational design contract**  
**Owner's standard:** Construct the collection as though it might be the last usable store of humanity's knowledge. **This is an aspiration and an acceptance criterion, not a claim that the existing collection already holds all human knowledge.**

## 1. The standard

A useful surviving library must allow future readers—human or machine—to recover **what a proposition means**, **why it was believed**, **how its supporting observation was obtained**, **how to reproduce the calculation or experiment**, **where its limits lie**, and **how it interacts with knowledge elsewhere**. Merely saving abstracts, embedding vectors, URLs, or a popular conclusion cannot preserve a scientific civilization's reasoning. An equation without symbols and units can be useless or dangerous; a measured number without instrument, sample and conditions is not transferable; a source summary without attribution cannot be corrected when the original disappears.

The library must work in stages of resource scarcity. At the lowest level a reader has only a filesystem and a UTF-8 text viewer. The next level has a standard Python interpreter and a modest CPU. Higher levels may use relational databases, specialized solvers, GPU models and search indexes. No critical theorem, provenance declaration, or document location may *require* proprietary cloud authentication, a vector index, or a language model to be decoded. Bitwork-powered routing, when built at the end of the 30-domain content program, will accelerate retrieval but must not be the only path to knowledge.

## 2. Every domain's minimum scientific package

A finished content domain needs original explanatory chapters and navigable source cards, but **volume is insufficient**. A chapter should cover: the question, definitions and physical/mathematical objects, variables and units, relevant historical foundations, assumptions, derivation or causal mechanism, observation/experiment or computational method, an independently reproducible numerical or procedural example, competing or contradictory views, uncertainty, invalid use cases, and connections to other chapters. Algorithms need versioned source code and known tests; a simulation must identify its assumptions and seed. A failure case is part of the knowledge, not noise to delete.

It should be possible for an apprentice to begin with basic prerequisites, build a working understanding, and tell what additional data would be needed to solve a real problem. **No fabricated confidence, novelty, measurement, or peer review.** If a gap prevents an answer, preserve the gap and the experiment that would resolve it. Verified, refuted, contradicted, plausible, and unknown claims remain distinguishable.

We preserve at least three distinct representations: readable **UTF-8 Markdown**, machine-readable **canonical JSON/JSONL** with explicit schema and qualified links, and executable **plain Python** or well-specified pseudocode where computational knowledge is essential. Tabular datasets should use documented field names, units, missing-value semantics and reproducible parsing. Non-text scientific media require an accessible descriptive equivalent (equations, alt text, dimensions and metadata) without falsely claiming the textual description reproduces their full data.

## 3. Evidence ranks are not ratings of truth

Evidence is recorded by access and validation capability, not compressed to one misleading percentage:

- **L0 — Citation or unresolved locator.** A source is named; its bytes and exact passages may be unavailable. Useful as a lead, **not independently recoverable**.
- **L1 — Source-scoped notes.** A selected passage, metadata or publisher abstract was reviewed and paraphrased. Original file still may be missing and rights remain external.
- **L2 — Locally retained source bytes with provenance and permitted rights.** Include canonical origin, retrieval date, SHA-256, parser version, license and exact inspected portion. A hash proves bytes, not accuracy.
- **L3 — Reproduced analysis against an authentic documented dataset.** Include original raw bytes where legally permitted, transformation scripts, outputs and discrepancies; still distinct from conducting the original laboratory experiment.
- **L4 — Independently corroborated science.** Separate scientific teams, data or high-quality methodological reviews support the claim under specified conditions; evidence and disagreement must be retained. This cannot be conferred by passing unit tests.

These levels describe *specific claims and sources*, not entire domains. A repository may have L3 mathematical code and only L0 primary research. **Never display L2–L4 without the applicable supporting files and reviewer attribution.** A paper's two different webpages may be one experiment; don't count them as two replications. Raw observations are retained separately from normalized derived values.

## 4. Reproducibility and recovery requirements

Every tagged release should have a self-consistent tree manifest, hash list, schemas, environment requirements, tests, known failures, an offline reading index and a `RESTORE` guide. Preserve immutable previous revisions and add explicit supersession/contradiction relationships instead of editing history to look consistent. The release should be extracted on a clean machine and verified without network access. A knowledge reader must be able to inspect the original chapters even when packages cannot install. A Python-only audit should enumerate source gaps, verify hashes and report potential dependence on online services.

Record the algorithms of human knowledge production: how to form hypotheses; design fair comparisons; calibrate an instrument; collect raw data; distinguish association from causation; quantify uncertainty; falsify claims; and update records after contradictory evidence. Preservation cannot be purely static. Scientific progress requires an *audit of corrections*.

Redundancy is not equal to backup. The final deployment plan should include three separately verified copies, more than one geographic/storage failure domain, periodic fixity checks, read-only archives, documented encryption/key custody where needed, durable software versions and periodic migration of fragile file formats. This program **has not implemented that final off-site archival infrastructure**; the charter specifies its eventual acceptance test. An untested copy is not a verified restore.

## 5. Human and AI pathways

For a human: start with a domain map, prerequisites, explanation, practical worked example, full source citation and failure scenarios. For an AI: return a **bounded citation package** containing a claim, exact source and revision, units and applicability, assumptions and uncertainty, contradiction links and abstention reason. A retrieval score is not scientific credibility. Retrieved documents remain untrusted data, never executable tool instructions or permission grants. A useful result can be **we don't know**, together with a testable plan to learn it.

Memory and self-improvement must be evaluated on independently authored held-out tasks, with precommitted rubrics and budgets, appropriate baseline, truthful uncertain outcomes, effect size and cost, plus tests against contamination and regressions. A cached answer that sounds convincing does not establish learning. Do not use this archive's own teaching tests as a hidden benchmark for models it has taught.

## 6. Build order and discipline

The thirty-domain roadmap governs **content order**. Signal and Cymatics are preserved companion nodes; Measurement, Causal, Materials, Control and Agent are the content domains already built to differing maturity. Subsequent domains will build their complete depth before being considered ready. Agent Atlas is moving toward a scoped v1, followed by Computation Atlas. **Bitwork routing comes only after all thirty domain libraries' content editions**, then comparative integration/evaluation and desktop transfer. Do not opportunistically change the router during corpus authoring. The shared Forge contract must remain versioned and source-closed.

Release labels are always two-dimensional: `content_scope_ready` and `scientific_or_deployment_accepted`. We can call the bounded content edition 1.0 while still honestly retaining unresolved independent review, third-party source acquisition, and live AI measurements. The survival standard means each remaining dependency is **visible and recoverable**, not silently presumed solved.

## 7. What this archive cannot yet claim

As of this charter, it is **not humanity's comprehensive knowledge**, is **not offline-complete for the primary sources it cites**, is **not independently peer-reviewed**, and has **not deployed its final cross-domain routing or distributed backup system**. In particular, many source cards link to web material whose original bytes are not archived. This is a critical survivability debt, not a superficial TODO. Resolve it with appropriately licensed copies, durable identifiers, bibliographic facts and original explanatory reconstruction where reuse rights are restricted.

**Acceptance phrase:** *Every important conclusion must survive with its proof path, limits, and a way to learn more—not merely its words.*

This charter is original project policy, not an international preservation standard or proof of completeness.
