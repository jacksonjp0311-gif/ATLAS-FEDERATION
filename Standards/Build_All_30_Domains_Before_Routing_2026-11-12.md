The owner’s current instruction is **finish all thirty domain knowledge libraries first; implement and evaluate the routing/Bitwork system last; transfer/install the complete collection on the owner's computer only after that**. This supersedes older sequencing restrictions, not the evidence requirements of any scientific release.

## Milestones

1. For each of the **30** candidate domains in `DOMAIN_QUEUE.json`, build a substantive bounded reference with domain-specific source maps, definitions, calculations where useful, worked case studies, failure cases, and explicit coverage limits. Do not confuse a proposed node with a built scientific reference.
2. Record a separate `content_ready` and `scientific_final_accepted` state. Content-ready may precede independent review/host validation, but never silently mark them passed.
3. Preserve one common source-closure/context contract and one cross-domain interface. Avoid hand-coding domain-dependent router logic during the content stage. Existing routing implementation stays frozen.
4. After the 30 domains' **content** editions are built, freeze a snapshot of their exact IDs, revisions, source references and rights limits.
5. Only then develop and benchmark the Bitwork-driven router against lexical/BM25 and semantic baselines on the same immutable corpus, recorded model and budget, independent held-out questions, costs and failure traces. Do not invent favorable performance.
6. Complete supported-host acceptance and consolidate for desktop transfer only after the domain collection and routing work.
