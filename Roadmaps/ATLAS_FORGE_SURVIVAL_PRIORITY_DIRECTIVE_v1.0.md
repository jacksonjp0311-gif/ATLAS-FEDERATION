# ATLAS FORGE — SURVIVAL-FIRST PRIORITY DIRECTIVE v1.0

**Issue date: 14 November 2026** · **Decision class:** owner-requested reprioritization, not a new scientific release.

**Executive decision: COVERAGE FIRST.** We will not keep polishing advanced domain manuals while safe water, healthcare, food, shelter and basic chemistry lack their own reconstruction references. The existing domain sciences and versions remain preserved and may continue deepening after the life-support seed shelf exists.

## 1. Reality check from the current records

- The original roadmap contains **30 named domain candidates**, **eight with existing nodes** and **22 not yet built**; Signal and Cymatics are two additional companion nodes, yielding ten existing nodes.
- Six of the 30 have scoped reference-content v1 and two (Measurement and Control) retain candidate/acceptance gates. Content readiness is not peer review or safety certification.
- The most recent completed checkpoint is **Energy Atlas v1.0.0**, with ten federation nodes, **2,052 records**, **1,245 relationships**, and **466 source cards**.
- The strict source audit has authenticated **zero original publisher files**. Original human-authored explanatory chapters exist, but link-only primary-source cards would fail a loss-of-internet test.
- The old next-domain target was **Bioelectric Atlas**. It remains valuable, but it does not replace foundational health, drinking water, sanitation or agriculture.

## 2. New governing rule: a preservation deadline exists at every moment

The goal is to leave a **useful, self-consistent surviving library if work stops after any future release**, not to maximize chapter counts or achieve a polished version label for one specialty at the expense of essential missing knowledge.

A basic domain release must survive with a low-dependency human lesson, units, definitions, derivations or evidence-checked procedures, source provenance and rights, known failure conditions, reconstruction examples, and an offline restore path. The first P0 edition may be called a *seed*, not v1; a medical, chemical, water, construction or electrical lesson cannot be called field-safe from software tests alone.

The priority list below is **editorial planning**, not an estimated lives-saved model. Priorities reflect direct survival relevance, dependency value for recovery, reliance on currently fragile sources, and speed of producing useful coverage.

## 6. What happens to the original 30-domain contract?

All thirty original node IDs remain in the canonical queue unchanged. This priority overlay explicitly ranks **every one of the 22 unbuilt original domains** and identifies **six urgent missing disciplines**. We have **not silently renamed or deleted original subjects**. For the new six we should create high-priority *seed workstreams*; before committing them as permanent domain slots, we must publish a separate, auditable coverage remapping or approve expansion beyond 30 named domains. In the meantime, a water/sanitation seed can exist as a human-readable knowledge packet and later become its own node.

This approach preserves the established **all domain knowledge content before Bitwork routing** rule. A reading index and prioritization document are *not* the adaptive router, and no new retrieval execution layer is authorized by this directive.

## 7. New execution rules

### Rule A — Protect source bytes first

At the next safe opportunity, prioritize legally distributable primary sources in the five immediate life-support areas. Preserve original bytes where rights permit, exact publisher/edition, retrieval date, checksum, license, original-versus-transformed relation, and repeated offline restoration evidence. A publisher URL is not an archive. Zero authenticated local originals among 466 citations is a major recovery risk that must appear on every research report until resolved.

### Rule B — Ship the survival shelf before more specialized v1 versions

The first concrete package should contain **short, accurately bounded coverage of water/sanitation, public health, food/nutrition, shelter, applied chemistry, repair/manufacturing, logistics, energy continuity, ecology, and communications**. Deliver each subject at least in source-linked human-readable form before building large numbers of specialized AI chapters. Preserve the basic training sequence, from measurement, safety and fundamentals to field-verified application.

### Rule C — Require useful reconstruction, not narrative volume

A seed contains (1) a purpose and definition, (2) prerequisites and sources, (3) math/physical units or evidence-qualified clinical categories, (4) one independently reconstructible example if appropriate, (5) failures and clear uncertainty, (6) rights and original-source access, (7) plain UTF-8 form, (8) an offline inventory/restore method. Safety-critical instructions require competent review before being described as operational. Unsupported therapeutic, food, electrical or chemical procedures are not authorized.

### Rule D — Report completion as a matrix

Track independently: **coverage seed ready**, **source bytes available and licensed**, **software/mathematics verified**, **human/domain review**, **real-field validation**, **offline restoration**. A domain is not “survival ready” merely because tests pass or a PDF exists.

### Rule E — Limit duplication

Existing Energy, Materials, Measurement, Control, Causal and Computation material supplies prerequisites. A new Water Atlas should link existing energy for pumping power and materials for corrosion, but must contribute original knowledge about water quality, sanitation and verification rather than copying unrelated code. Bioelectric’s eventual specialist research should link to Public Health and Biological Systems without pretending to be a primary medical-care manual.

## 9. Source standards supporting the triage

The official [Sphere Handbook](https://spherestandards.org/handbook/) covers four directly life-critical response sectors: WASH, food security and nutrition, shelter and settlement, and health. Sphere states its 2018 Handbook remains official until the next planned edition in 2028. The framework prioritizes dignity, community participation and coordinated systems, not isolated gadgets.

The [WHO 2026 drinking-water quality guidelines](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240121225) emphasize water-safety plans, contamination-risk identification and independent surveillance. The [WHO essential medicines lists](https://www.who.int/groups/expert-committee-on-selection-and-Use-of-essential-medicines/essential-medicines-lists) address priority health needs, not do-it-yourself drug manufacture. [FAO](https://www.fao.org/policy-support/policy-themes/emergencies-and-rural-livelihoods/en) emphasizes protecting farming livelihoods and food production during emergencies. The [Library of Congress Recommended Formats Statement](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/resources/rfs/) supplies long-term format guidance. Rights must be checked for each source before an offline copy is distributed.
